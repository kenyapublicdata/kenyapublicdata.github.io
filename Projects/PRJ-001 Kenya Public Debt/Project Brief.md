# Project Brief

## Problem

Kenya's public debt is commonly compared across administrations using nominal shilling totals. Those comparisons omit changes in the value of money, economic size, population, exchange rates, debt composition, and fiscal capacity.

## Intended Contribution

Create a transparent comparison that shows several valid but distinct views of the debt rather than treating one number as the complete story.

## Initial Scope

- Total public debt over the longest defensible consistent period.
- Nominal and inflation-adjusted local-currency values.
- Debt as a share of GDP.
- Domestic and external components where comparable data permit.
- Administration periods as descriptive annotations, not automatic causal explanations.
- Debt-service burden as a subsequent module if the necessary series can be reconciled.

## Out of Scope for the First Publication

- Proving that a particular president caused every change during an administration.
- Evaluating the quality of every debt-financed project.
- Estimating unreported, contingent, county, or state-corporation liabilities unless they are consistently included in the selected official definition.
- Ranking administrations with a single composite score.

## Audience

Kenyans seeking understandable public-finance context, journalists, researchers, civic organizations, policymakers, and students.

## Editorial Posture

Show first. Interpret second. Explain why different measures answer different questions, disclose limitations, and avoid partisan framing.
