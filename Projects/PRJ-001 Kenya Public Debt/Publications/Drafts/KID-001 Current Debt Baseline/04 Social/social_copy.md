## 1. LinkedIn & Long-Form Professional Post

**Headline:** Kenya’s Public Debt Baseline: What the Official Numbers Actually Show

Kenya’s public debt debate is frequently characterized by large nominal totals and polarizing claims. To establish a transparent, evidence-based baseline, here is what the latest official reports from the National Treasury and the Central Bank of Kenya show regarding the current stock, composition, and debt-servicing burden:

📊 **The Current Debt Stock**
- **June 2025 (Fiscal Baseline):** Gross public and publicly guaranteed (PPG) debt stood at **KSh 11.814 Trillion** (67.8% of GDP).
- **May 2026 (Latest Complete CBK Total):** Public debt reached **KSh 12.896 Trillion**, an increase of KSh 1.08 Trillion (+9.2%) over 11 months.

🔄 **Domestic vs. External Shift**
- Domestic borrowing now forms the majority of Kenya's debt portfolio: **53.5%** in June 2025 and **56.1%** by May 2026.
- While domestic debt shields the budget from direct foreign exchange depreciation, it carries substantial fiscal carrying costs.

💸 **The Debt Servicing Squeeze**
- In FY 2024/25, total debt service was **KSh 1.722 Trillion**, consuming **71.2% of ordinary revenue** (tax and recurring non-tax revenue).
- **The primary driver is domestic interest:** Domestic interest payments alone absorbed **KSh 776.3 Billion (32.1% of ordinary revenue)**, consuming nearly four times more revenue than external interest (KSh 211.2 Billion / 8.7%).

⚖️ **Statutory Anchors & Risk**
- **Present Value of Debt:** 63.7% of GDP at June 2025, exceeding the 55% statutory debt anchor set in Section 50(2A) of the Public Finance Management Act by 8.7 percentage points.
- **Risk Assessment:** The latest joint IMF–World Bank Debt Sustainability Analysis classifies Kenya at **high risk of debt distress**, though debt remains sustainable on a finely balanced basis.
