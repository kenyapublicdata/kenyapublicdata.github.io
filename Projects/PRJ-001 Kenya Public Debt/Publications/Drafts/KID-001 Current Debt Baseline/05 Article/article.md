# Kenya's Public Debt: The Current Stock, Composition and Servicing Burden

> **Executive Summary:** At end-June 2025, Kenya's gross public and publicly guaranteed (PPG) debt stood at **KSh 11.814 trillion**, equivalent to **67.8% of GDP in nominal terms** and **63.7% in present-value (PV) terms**. Domestic debt accounted for **53.5%** of the total stock. By May 2026—the latest complete monthly total published by the Central Bank of Kenya—the debt stock had expanded by KSh 1.08 trillion (+9.2%) to **KSh 12.896 trillion**, with the domestic share climbing further to **56.1%**.
> 
> In FY 2024/25, total debt service reached **KSh 1.722 trillion**, consuming **71.2% of ordinary revenue** and **39.0% of total public expenditure**. The immediate fiscal squeeze is driven primarily by domestic borrowing costs: domestic interest payments alone absorbed **KSh 776.3 billion** (32.1% of ordinary revenue), exceeding total external debt service.

---

## 1. Reading the Dates and Definitions

Understanding Kenya's public debt requires distinguishing between two official observation dates and understanding the scope of official reporting:

1. **End-June 2025 Fiscal Baseline:** Sourced from the National Treasury's retrospective *Annual Public Debt Management Report FY 2024/2025*. This provides comprehensive, audited outturns for macroeconomic ratios, debt composition, and annual debt service flows against government revenues.
2. **May 2026 Latest Monthly Update:** Sourced from the Central Bank of Kenya's *Monthly Economic Indicators (June 2026)*. This reports the latest complete simultaneous observation of both domestic and external debt stocks.
   *(Note: While CBK Table 7.2 reports a domestic debt figure of KSh 7,327.8 billion for June 2026, external debt for June 2026 was not yet published in that edition; combining June domestic debt with May external debt would create an inconsistent hybrid).*

### Gross vs. Net Debt Coverage
Kenya's official headline debt statistics report **gross public and publicly guaranteed (PPG) debt**. Gross debt records the total outstanding contractual liabilities of the national government before deducting any liquid financial assets (such as government bank deposits or sinking fund balances). Because the National Treasury and CBK do not publish a standardized net-debt time series, this release uses official gross PPG debt.

## 4. The Debt-Servicing Squeeze

While headline debt stock measures total accumulated obligations, the most acute operational constraint on the national budget is **debt service**—the annual cash required to pay interest and redeem principal.

In FY 2024/25, total public debt service was **KSh 1,722.1 billion**.

### Fiscal Absorption Ratios (FY 2024/25)

| Metric | Result | Fiscal Interpretation |
|:---|---:|:---|
| **Debt Service / Ordinary Revenue** | **71.2%** | Debt service consumed KSh 71.20 of every KSh 100 in ordinary revenue collected. |
| **Total Interest / Ordinary Revenue** | **40.8%** | Pure carrying cost of debt without reducing principal balances. |
| **Domestic Interest / Ordinary Revenue** | **32.1%** | Domestic interest alone absorbed nearly a third of all ordinary revenue. |
| **External Interest / Ordinary Revenue** | **8.7%** | External carrying cost, reflecting concessional terms on multilateral loans. |
| **Debt Service / Total Public Expenditure** | **39.0%** | Debt service absorbed almost four out of every ten shillings spent by government. |
| **Debt Service / Nominal GDP** | **9.9%** | Total annual cash debt servicing flow relative to national economic output. |

> **Accounting Clarification:** In official Treasury reporting, *ordinary revenue* includes tax and recurring non-tax revenue (KSh 2,420.2B in FY 2024/25) and is broader than KRA tax collections alone. In addition, the domestic principal figure excludes routine roll-overs of short-term Treasury bills.

### Anatomy of Annual Debt Service: Interest vs. Principal

Of the **KSh 1,722.1 billion** paid in debt service during FY 2024/25:
- **Interest Payments:** **KSh 987.5 billion (57.3% of total service)**
  - Domestic Interest: **KSh 776.3 billion** (78.6% of all interest paid)
  - External Interest: **KSh 211.2 billion** (21.4% of all interest paid)
- **Principal Repayments (Amortization):** **KSh 734.6 billion (42.7% of total service)**
  - External Principal Amortization: **KSh 367.8 billion**
  - Domestic Bond Redemptions: **KSh 366.8 billion** *(excluding T-bill rollovers)*

## 5. Macroeconomic Scale & Statutory Rules

### Debt-to-GDP: Nominal (67.8%) vs Present Value (63.7%)
- **Nominal Debt-to-GDP (67.8%):** Compares total contractual face value against annual GDP (KSh 17,434.5B).
- **Present Value Debt-to-GDP (63.7%):** Discounts future debt flows using concessional grant elements. Because multilateral loans carry below-market interest rates and long grace periods, the present value is lower than nominal face value.

### The 55% Statutory Debt Anchor
Section 50(2A) of the **Public Finance Management Act (PFMA 2012)** sets Kenya's national borrowing threshold at **55% of GDP in present-value terms**, with Section 50(2B) permitting a temporary deviation band of up to **5 percentage points** (up to 60.0%).

- At **63.7% of GDP in PV terms**, Kenya's debt stood **8.7 percentage points above the statutory 55% anchor** and **3.7 percentage points above the top of the exceptional allowance band**.

### Debt Distress Assessment
Under the joint **IMF–World Bank Debt Sustainability Analysis (DSA)** dated October 2024 (IMF Country Report No. 24/316):
- **External Risk of Debt Distress:** High
- **Overall Risk of Debt Distress:** High
- **Debt Sustainability:** Sustainable on a *finely balanced basis*, conditional on sustained fiscal consolidation and export growth.

## 6. Structural Dynamics of the Shift Toward Domestic Debt

Domestic debt expanded from **48.0%** of total debt in FY 2020/21 to **53.5%** in June 2025, and reached **56.1%** by May 2026. This structural shift embodies significant macroeconomic trade-offs:

### The Trade-off Matrix

| Dimension | Favourable Effects | Macroeconomic Costs and Vulnerabilities |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Currency Risk** | Eliminates direct foreign-exchange vulnerability on that portion of debt; shilling depreciation does not mechanically inflate domestic obligations. | None directly on debt stock, but does not shield external obligations. |
| **Fiscal Carrying Cost** | Supports domestic financial market development and local capital mobilization. | **High Interest Cost:** Domestic debt is 53.5% of stock but generates **78.6% of total interest**. Domestic interest alone consumes 32.1% of ordinary revenue. |
| **Private Sector Credit** | Provides secure, liquid yields for pension funds and insurance companies. | **Crowding Out:** Commercial banks held **34.7% of domestic debt** at June 2025, incentivizing lending to the sovereign over private SMEs. |
| **Refinancing Horizon** | Settled and traded within the domestic legal system. | **Rollover Risk:** Treasury bills rose 68% in FY 2024/25; 21.7% of domestic debt matured within 12 months, creating high rollover frequency. |

---

## 7. Key Strategic Takeaways

1. **The Core Pressure is Liquidity, Not Stock Alone:** With debt service consuming **71.2% of ordinary revenue**, debt servicing obligations leave only 28.8% of ordinary revenue for non-debt recurrent expenditure, county equitable share transfers, and development programs.
2. **Domestic Borrowing Drives the Interest Bill:** While external borrowing carries currency risk, domestic borrowing is the dominant driver of fiscal cost, absorbing nearly four times as much interest as external debt.
3. **Rigid Expenditure Concentration:** The National Treasury reports that debt service, pensions, constitutional salaries, and county transfers combined accounted for **63.9% of total expenditure** in FY 2024/25, reducing discretionary development expenditure to ~12%.
4. **Statutory Alignment Remains Pending:** At 63.7% PV of GDP, debt remains above the 55% statutory threshold set out in the PFM Act.
