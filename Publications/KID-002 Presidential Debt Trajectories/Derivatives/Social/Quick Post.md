# KID-002 — Quick Post (Audited Baseline)

Derived from: [`KID-002 Presidential Debt Trajectories.md`](../../KID-002%20Presidential%20Debt%20Trajectories.md).

## Primary caption

**Kenya's public debt across presidencies: comparing the first 3 years**

When adjusted for inflation using official KNBS Consumer Price Index data (Constant 2024 KSh) and evaluated on matched National Treasury historical series:

🇰🇪 **Mwai Kibaki (Dec 2002 – Jun 2005):**
• Real debt: KSh 3.29T ➔ KSh 3.04T (📉 **-7.5% real contraction**)
• Debt-to-GDP: **64.0% ➔ 55.6%** (-8.4 percentage points)
• Debt service/Ordinary revenue: **35.7% ➔ 27.1%**

🇰🇪 **Uhuru Kenyatta (Jun 2013 – Jun 2016):**
• Real debt: KSh 3.70T ➔ KSh 5.83T (📈 **+57.5% real surge**)
• Debt-to-GDP: **42.1% ➔ 54.8%** (+12.7 percentage points)
• Debt service/Ordinary revenue: **28.2% ➔ 35.5%**

🇰🇪 **William Ruto (Jun 2022 – Jun 2025):**
• Real debt: KSh 9.87T ➔ KSh 11.31T (+14.5% real; **+KSh 3.05T nominal**)
• Debt-to-GDP: **68.7% ➔ 67.8%** (-0.9 pp; PV debt at 63.7% vs 55% anchor)
• Debt service/Ordinary revenue: **47.9% ➔ 71.2%** (KSh 1.722T service)

**Key takeaway:** Kibaki grew the economy faster than debt. Uhuru presided over the steepest real debt acceleration (Eurobonds & China Exim SGR loans). Under Ruto, Debt-to-GDP held flat, but debt servicing exploded from 47.9% to 71.2% of ordinary revenue due to domestic interest costs.

**Data:** KNBS CPI (official series Feb 2019=100; rebased to 2024=100); National Treasury ADMR Reports.
