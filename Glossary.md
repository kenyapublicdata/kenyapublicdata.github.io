# Kenya in Data Glossary

## Purpose

This is the shared vocabulary for all Kenya in Data projects. It defines recurring economic, fiscal, statistical, and publishing terms in plain language and records how Kenya in Data uses them.

Project notes should link here instead of independently redefining common concepts. A project may add a narrower operational definition when its source or method requires one, but it must explain the difference.

## Definition Standard

Each important term should eventually record:

- a plain-language definition;
- the precise Kenya in Data convention;
- unit and calculation where relevant;
- the responsible source or statistical standard;
- terms with which it is commonly confused;
- known source-specific or historical variations.

Definitions in this glossary explain language. The definition attached to a specific indicator and source remains authoritative for an individual calculation.

## General Data and Measurement

### Actual

An observation reported as having occurred, rather than an estimate or forecast. “Actual” does not necessarily mean audited or final; the source's status must still be recorded.

### Average

A value calculated across several observations or across a period. A monthly average exchange rate is different from the rate on the final day of the month.

### Base period or base year

The period against which an index or constant-price series is expressed. A base-year value is often set to 100, or prices from that year are used to express other years in comparable units.

Changing a base year can alter the level and historical structure of a series. Record the base year and never splice differently based series without an explicit method.

### Break in series

A point at which observations before and after are not directly comparable because definitions, coverage, classification, methodology, data sources, or base years changed.

### Calendar year

The period from 1 January to 31 December. Do not silently compare a calendar-year measure with a fiscal-year measure.

### Constant prices

Values expressed using the prices of a selected base period so that changes over time are not driven solely by general price changes. Often described as “real” values.

### Current prices

Values measured using the prices prevailing in each observation period. Often described as “nominal” values.

### Data vintage

The specific edition of a dataset available at a particular time. Later vintages may revise earlier observations. Published Kenya in Data work should record the vintage used.

### End of period

The value observed on or at the end of a reporting period. Debt stock is often reported at month-end, quarter-end, or fiscal-year-end.

### Estimate

A value calculated when a final observation is not yet available. Estimates should be visibly distinguished from actual observations and projections.

### Fiscal year

The government's accounting year. Kenya's national fiscal year runs from 1 July to 30 June and is written, for example, as `FY 2024/25`.

### Flow

An amount measured over a period, such as annual revenue, borrowing, interest payments, or expenditure.

### Index

A measure that expresses values relative to a reference period, often set to 100. An index shows relative change; it does not necessarily show a currency amount.

### Nominal value

A value reported in the money or prices of the observation period, without removing the effect of general price changes.

For debt, “nominal” may also refer to the contractual or face amount. Kenya in Data must state whether it means an unadjusted current-price total, face value, market value, or another source-specific valuation.

### Observation date

The exact date or period to which a value refers. It is distinct from the date on which the source document was published or retrieved.

### Percentage change

The relative change from an earlier value:

`percentage change = (new value − old value) ÷ old value × 100`

### Percentage point

The arithmetic difference between two percentages. A debt-to-GDP ratio moving from 50% to 55% rises by **5 percentage points**, equivalent to a **10% relative increase** in the ratio.

### Per capita

An amount divided by the relevant population. Per-capita debt is an allocation of the debt stock across residents for comparison; it is not a personal bill legally owed by each resident.

### Projection or forecast

A value describing a future period based on assumptions or a model. It must not be presented as an observed result.

### Real value

A nominal value adjusted using a specified price index so that amounts from different periods are expressed in comparable purchasing-value terms.

“Real debt” does not by itself measure affordability, fiscal space, or sustainability. Those questions require denominators such as GDP or revenue and information about servicing terms.

### Revision

A change made by the source institution to a previously published observation. A source revision is different from a Kenya in Data correction and should be labelled accordingly.

### Stock

An amount measured at a point in time, such as debt outstanding on 30 June. A stock should not be directly added to a yearly flow without a meaningful reason and compatible units.

## Prices, Inflation, and Economic Scale

### Consumer Price Index — CPI

An index measuring changes over time in the prices paid for a defined basket of consumer goods and services.

CPI adjustment answers a household-purchasing-value question. It does not necessarily represent changes in the prices of everything produced by the economy or purchased by government.

### Deflation or inflation adjustment

The conversion of a nominal series into constant-price terms using a selected price index.

A common conversion is:

`real value in base-period prices = nominal value × (price index in base period ÷ price index in observation period)`

The selected index and base period must always be disclosed.

### GDP deflator

An index reflecting the prices of domestically produced final goods and services included in GDP. It is commonly derived from nominal GDP relative to real GDP.

The GDP deflator has broader economic coverage than CPI, but historical rebasing and revisions must be treated carefully.

### Gross Domestic Product — GDP

The monetary value of final goods and services produced within an economy over a period. GDP is a flow, not a stock.

Nominal GDP is measured at current prices. Real GDP removes the effect of price changes using national-accounts methods.

### Inflation

A sustained increase in the general price level, commonly measured by the percentage change in CPI or another price index. Inflation reduces what a fixed nominal amount can purchase, but different price indices answer different questions.

## Public Finance

### Budget deficit or fiscal deficit

The shortfall when government expenditure exceeds revenue and grants over a period, using the accounting definition specified by the source.

A deficit is a flow. Public debt is a stock accumulated through past borrowing, valuation changes, recognition of liabilities, and other adjustments.

### Development expenditure

Government spending classified for development or capital purposes under the applicable budget framework. The classification does not by itself demonstrate that spending created a productive asset or achieved value for money.

### Expenditure

Government spending recognized during a period under the stated accounting framework. Budgeted, committed, disbursed, and actual expenditure are different concepts.

### Fiscal space

The government's room to increase spending or reduce taxes without endangering fiscal sustainability or market access. Fiscal space cannot be inferred from the debt stock alone.

### Government revenue

Resources received by government, potentially including tax revenue, non-tax revenue, grants, and other receipts depending on the source.

Always state whether a ratio uses tax revenue, ordinary revenue, total revenue, or revenue and grants.

### Gross financing need

The total financing required to cover the fiscal deficit plus maturing debt and any other specified financing requirements during a period.

### Interest payment

The cost paid for using borrowed funds. Interest is distinct from repayment of principal.

### Ordinary revenue

Recurring government revenue from taxes and specified non-tax sources, excluding borrowing and generally excluding grants. Use the exact definition in the relevant Kenyan budget document.

### Primary balance

Government revenue minus non-interest expenditure over a period. A primary surplus means revenue exceeds expenditure before interest; a primary deficit means it does not.

### Recurrent expenditure

Government spending classified as recurring operational expenditure, such as compensation, operations, transfers, and interest, under the applicable budget framework.

### Tax revenue

Revenue collected through taxes. It is narrower than total revenue and must not be used interchangeably with ordinary revenue or revenue and grants.

## Public Debt

### Amortization

Repayment of loan principal according to the financing agreement. In some reports, “amortization” may refer specifically to scheduled external principal repayments.

### Arrears

Payments that were due but remain unpaid. Arrears may constitute liabilities without always appearing in the headline public-debt stock.

### Bilateral debt

Debt owed to another government or its official lending agency.

### Commercial debt

Borrowing on market-related terms from commercial lenders or investors, including international bonds and syndicated bank loans. The precise classification follows the reporting source.

### Concessional debt

Debt offered on more favourable terms than market borrowing, generally through lower interest rates, longer maturities, or grace periods. Concessionality should be assessed using the source's formal method rather than the interest rate alone.

### Contingent liability

A potential government obligation that becomes payable if a specified event occurs, such as the failure of a guaranteed borrower. It is not always included in headline debt until the obligation is recognized.

### Coupon

The contractual interest payment on a bond, usually expressed as a percentage of its face value. Coupon rate, current yield, and yield to maturity are different measures.

### Creditor

The person or institution to whom money is owed. Creditor categories may include multilateral institutions, foreign governments, banks, pension funds, insurers, investors, and other entities.

### Debt anchor

A medium- or long-term fiscal benchmark intended to guide public-debt policy, often expressed relative to GDP and sometimes in present-value terms. It is distinct from a fixed nominal debt ceiling.

### Debt ceiling or debt limit

A legal or policy maximum on government debt or borrowing. Its meaning depends on the applicable law, measurement basis, coverage, and date.

### Debt distress

A condition in which a government experiences, or is at high risk of experiencing, difficulty meeting debt obligations and may require exceptional financing, restructuring, or the accumulation of arrears.

### Debt service

Payments associated with debt obligations during a period. It commonly includes principal repayments and interest, but some documents use narrower definitions. Kenya in Data must state exactly which components are included.

### Debt-service-to-revenue ratio

Debt-service payments divided by the specified measure of government revenue. The numerator and denominator must cover compatible periods, and both “debt service” and “revenue” must be defined.

### Debt stock

The amount of debt outstanding at a specified point in time. Debt stock is distinct from new borrowing during a period and from annual debt-service payments.

### Debt sustainability

The government's capacity to meet current and future debt obligations without implausibly large policy adjustments, default, or exceptional financing. Sustainability depends on financing terms, revenue, growth, interest rates, exchange rates, maturity, risks, and policy—not only debt-to-GDP.

### Debt-to-GDP ratio

The debt stock divided by nominal GDP and expressed as a percentage. It scales debt relative to the size of the economy but does not directly measure annual repayment pressure or government revenue.

The stock date and GDP period must follow a disclosed, compatible convention.

### Domestic debt

Debt classified as domestic under the relevant reporting standard, often based on the residence of the creditor, the market of issuance, or the governing framework.

Domestic debt is not automatically identical to local-currency debt. Kenya in Data must preserve the source's classification and state the convention used.

### Eurobond

An international bond issued in a currency or market outside the issuer's domestic market. Kenya's sovereign Eurobonds are external commercial debt; “Eurobond” does not mean the bond is necessarily denominated in euros.

### Exchange-rate revaluation

A change in the domestic-currency value of foreign-currency debt caused by movement in exchange rates, even when the foreign-currency principal has not changed.

### External debt

Debt classified as external under the relevant reporting standard, commonly according to non-resident creditors or external issuance.

External debt is often denominated in foreign currency but is not conceptually identical to foreign-currency debt. The source's classification must be recorded.

### Face value

The principal amount stated on a debt instrument and used to calculate contractual repayment. It can differ from market value and present value.

### Foreign-currency debt

Debt whose contractual payments are denominated in a currency other than the Kenyan shilling. It creates direct exchange-rate exposure regardless of how the creditor is classified.

### Foreign debt

An informal and potentially ambiguous phrase. Kenya in Data should normally use the more precise term **external debt** or **foreign-currency debt**, depending on what is meant.

### Grace period

A period after a loan is contracted during which specified payments, often principal repayments, are not yet due.

### Gross debt

Total recognized debt liabilities before subtracting government financial assets or deposits specified by the source.

### Guarantee

A commitment by government to repay another entity's obligation if that entity fails to do so. Guaranteed debt may be reported separately from direct government debt.

### Maturity

The date on which a debt instrument's remaining principal is contractually due. “Time to maturity” measures the interval until that date.

### Multilateral debt

Debt owed to international institutions whose membership includes multiple countries, such as international or regional development and financial institutions.

### Net debt

Gross debt minus specified government financial assets or deposits. Definitions vary considerably; always state what has been subtracted.

### New borrowing

Debt contracted or disbursed during a period. It is not the same as the change in debt stock because repayments, exchange-rate movements, recognition changes, and other adjustments also affect the stock.

### Present value

The value today of future payments discounted using a stated rate. Present-value debt can differ from face-value debt, particularly for concessional loans.

### Principal

The underlying borrowed amount that must be repaid, excluding interest and fees.

### Public and publicly guaranteed debt — PPG debt

Direct public debt plus debt of other entities that carries an explicit public guarantee, under the institutional coverage specified by the source.

### Public debt

Debt obligations of government entities included within a stated institutional boundary. The term is not self-defining: coverage may refer to central government, general government, or a broader public sector and may include or exclude guarantees.

Every Kenya in Data publication must state the debt coverage it uses.

### Refinancing

Raising new financing to repay or replace existing debt. Refinancing can change interest cost, currency, maturity, or risk without necessarily financing new expenditure.

### Rollover risk

The risk that maturing debt cannot be refinanced, or can be refinanced only at substantially worse terms.

### Sovereign debt

Debt issued or owed by the national government. Depending on context, it may be narrower than the full public-sector debt measure.

### Treasury bill

A short-term government security, typically issued at a discount and repaid at face value at maturity.

### Treasury bond

A government security with a longer maturity, generally paying interest according to its contractual terms.

### Yield

The return implied by a security's price and cash flows. Coupon rate, auction yield, current yield, and yield to maturity should not be treated as synonyms.

## Interpretation Rules

1. Never use **nominal**, **real**, **current prices**, or **constant prices** without identifying the relevant period or price basis.
2. Never use **debt service** without stating whether principal is included.
3. Never use **government revenue** in a ratio without naming the revenue concept.
4. Never treat **external debt** and **foreign-currency debt** as automatic synonyms.
5. Never compare a **stock** directly with a **flow** without explaining the relationship.
6. Never mix fiscal-year, calendar-year, average-period, and end-period observations silently.
7. Never describe a projection as an actual observation.
8. Use **percentage points** for differences between ratios and **percentage change** for relative growth.
9. A per-capita debt figure is a comparison device, not an invoice to each resident.
10. Project-specific source definitions override generic shorthand and must be disclosed.
